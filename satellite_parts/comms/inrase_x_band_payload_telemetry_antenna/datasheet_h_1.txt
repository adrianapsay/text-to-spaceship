1 Overview 
The passive array microstrip antenna operates in the X-band frequency range, making it 
ideal for satellite communication applications requiring high throughput. Designed for small 
satellite applications, this antenna features a compact, lightweight construction ensuring efficient 
space utilization and ease of integration. The antenna leverages advanced microstrip technology 
and provides excellent gain and directivity, enhancing signal strength and quality for satellite 
communication systems. 
Features 
• Operates in the 8050-8400 MHz X-band allocation for Earth science satellites 
• Flat passive microstrip array  
• 
Designed to be integrated with ECS high-throughput X-band transmitter (PRDC) 
• Gain of 18.5 dB is 3 dB higher than typical competing antennas targeted for 1U 
formfactor, that provides corresponding link budget improvement 
• Test cap (test hat) is available for the satellite integration testing

3 Principles of Operation  
The X-band Antenna is a passive microstrip antenna array. Its operation is based on the 
principles of electromagnetic wave propagation and resonance. The array consists of a matrix of 
microstrip patch elements, each acting as a radiating element, printed on a dielectric substrate. 
These elements are fed by a network that distributes the input signal uniformly across the array, 
ensuring coherent radiation. The patches resonate at specific frequencies in the X-band, converting 
the guided electromagnetic waves into free-space radiation efficiently. The array configuration 
allows for constructive interference of the radiated waves, to enhance the overall gain and 
directivity. The passive nature of the antenna means it does not require active components, relying 
solely on the spatial arrangement and feeding network to achieve the desired radiation pattern and 
performance. 
The antenna is made from woven glass reinforced hydrocarbon/ceramics dielectric 
material, that complies with NASA outgassing requirements. Front side (“A” in Figure 9) features 
microstrip patch radiating elements, with immersion gold plating. The antenna backside (“B” in 
Figure 9) shall be mounted on the flat conducting surface of the satellite.  
The SMA connector ensures a reliable interface between the feeder cable and the antenna. 
Larger diameter and more rigid coaxial cables can be used.
