# M4: bracket layouts — one per part, combined per wall, one piece

Until M4 every part had its own bracket. M4 adds two more ways to hold the parts of a Stage 2 layout, so that the
three can be compared and one chosen:

| Layout | What it is | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **One bracket per part** | the design job of V2 (§4 of `docs/V2.md`) | unchanged, the default |
| **Combined per wall** | neighbouring metal 3D-printed brackets on one wall (or one corner rail) joined into one print | new |
| **One piece** | one print that holds every part except the PC/104 boards | new, **exploratory** |

The user's rule for both new layouts: *a bracket may hold several parts, but no part may be held only by another
part* — every part must reach the chassis through bracket material.

## In the app

**Mounting Hardware → Bracket layout** shows the three side by side, one column each:

* bracket mass in total (every bracket of the layout, fasteners included; the PC/104 rods and standoffs are the
  same in every layout and left out);
* the worst margin of safety and its check (the bracket and parts it belongs to);
* the system's first natural frequency (the lowest over the brackets with the parts they hold);
* the number of printed pieces and the largest one's size, against the L-PBF build volume, **250 × 250 × 325 mm
  (ASSUMED: a typical metal L-PBF machine)**;
* which parts each print holds (for combined, the plan before the run).

**Design** opens the settings of a run — material, material used (automatic ladder or one value), minimum member
thickness, and for the one piece a time budget per material-use step (1–120 min) and **Estimate time and memory**,
which builds the one piece's design space and shows the grid, the free voxels, the predicted time and memory, and the
memory available now, before anything heavy starts. The run goes in the background with the same progress bars as
the per-part design (one row per combined bracket; the one piece's phase), and only one design run of any kind runs
at a time. The per-part designs stay.

**Use for saved STL files** chooses which layout the app shows and saves: the 3D views, the physics (mass, centre of
mass), the bill of materials, the drawings, the fit proof and the saved STL files (`saved_stl/manifest.json` records
`bracket_layout`) follow the choice. Per part stays the default; nothing changes until the user chooses.

API: `GET /api/layouts/{id}/bracket-layouts`, `POST …/bracket-layouts/one_piece/estimate`,
`POST …/bracket-layouts/{mode}/run`, `POST …/bracket-layouts/use` (`docs/api-contract.md`). The mock backend answers
"not available" for these (they need the real optimiser and solvers).

## Combined per wall — how it is built

* **Which brackets:** metal 3D-printed brackets fixed to the same target (the same wall or end-plate plane, or the
  same corner rail). Direct mounts, machined blocks, sheet brackets, clamps and cradles keep their per-part form.
* **Bridges:** two such brackets are joined by a bridging box across the gap between their reserved boxes — straight,
  over the width both boxes share (at least 3 mm, ASSUMED), across a gap of at most 25 mm (ASSUMED: "neighbouring"),
  from the wall to the nearer part side. A bridge must keep 1.2 mm (the packing's bracket clearance, plus its 2 µm
  rounding allowance) from every other part, every other reserved box, every keep-out except the target rail, and
  every bridge of another group. Closest pairs first; joined brackets form groups.
* **Design region:** the member boxes and the bridges, snapped to the voxel grid (the bridge reaches one voxel into
  each member). The region is eroded at re-entrant corners until the voxels of every 2 × 2 × 2 block lie in one box.
  Then every marching-cubes triangle lies in one box, and because all boxes are columns standing on one plane the
  solid lies in their union. **The fit proof (Lemma 4) now accepts a bracket made of several boxes**: the certificate
  lists the boxes (each a frame item) and the solid's triangles, and `tools/check_certificate.py` (check 10) checks
  that the surface is closed (every edge in exactly two triangles, once each way), that every triangle has its three
  vertices in one of the boxes, and that every box starts on the target plane and extends to the same side; a member
  box is exempt for its own part only, a bridge for its two end parts, and every box must be apart from every other
  bracket's boxes and the keep-outs (the box arithmetic of Lemma 4).
* **Voxel size:** the finest of its members' per-part sizes (1.0–1.5 mm; coarser if the union's grid would exceed
  400 000 voxels). If the FE check would not fit the memory left, the bracket is designed again on voxels 0.25 mm
  larger (up to 2 mm); every attempt is listed.
* **Each held part** keeps its own contact zone (inside its own box), its mass, centre of mass and inertia, and its
  own bolt pattern on its own bolted face.
* **The load-path rule** is checked twice: on the design (every contact voxel of every part must be joined to the
  bolted layer through bracket voxels, not through another part — else the design is refused with that reason), and
  on the final mesh (one connected solid on the bolted face, with every part's load faces on it).
* **The check** (`gd.multi_eval`): quadratic tetrahedra on exactly the certified surface. NGSolve solves every case
  with one factorisation: the three 68 g cases with all parts loaded at once, then per part three forces and three
  torques alone, and the three self-weight cases (Ritz trial vectors: 6 per part + 3). CalculiX solves the three
  68 g cases as the independent cross-check. No CalculiX `*RIGID BODY` or `*ROTARY INERTIA` is used: the parts are
  rigid masses only in the Ritz mass matrix. Modes and the random, sine and shock responses as for one part. The
  score is given for the whole bracket and for each held part's own region (its box and its bolts).
* **Settings** (material, material used, minimum member thickness) apply per combined bracket.
* **Where the surface touches a part:** a marching-cubes triangle can cut through the corner of a void voxel next
  to a part. Those triangles are found (FCL) and the void corners of their grid cubes lowered until no triangle cuts
  the part — the surface moves back into the solid voxels and no voxel changes side. Subtracting the part (the
  per-part designs' safety net) is kept only as the last resort, because the cut leaves slivers that the
  tetrahedral mesher cannot mesh (seen on the one piece below).

## One piece — how it is built (exploratory)

* **Grid:** one grid over the inner box, lying exactly on all six inner planes, voxels ≤ 2.7 mm across the side
  walls (so the 3 mm mounting gap of every part holds one voxel layer with ≥ 0.3 mm to the part) and ≤ 4 mm along
  the length.
* **Bolted where the per-part brackets were** (a choice): on each part's own wall or end plate, under its footprint,
  with that bracket's fasteners. Parts on a corner rail keep their per-part bracket.
* **Free space:** everything inside the inner envelope except 1.2 mm around every keep-out (rails, PC/104 rods and
  stack reserve, harness, standoffs, brackets that stay) and every part it does not hold — measured from the cube of
  twice the voxel around each voxel, which contains every marching-cubes triangle of that voxel — and 1.2 mm from
  every held part outside its own contact zone and mounting gap.
* **Optimiser:** the same SIMP as the combined brackets, on the active voxels only (one sparse factorisation per
  iteration), coarse-to-fine (2 × 2 × 2 blocks first), within the time budget. Before the fine level it checks the
  memory: if the fine level would not fit, the coarse level's design is the result (said so).
* **One print:** the solid is joined into one piece by shortest paths through the free space; a design that still
  falls apart is refused.
* **Proof** (check 10 of `tools/check_certificate.py`): the surface is closed, every vertex lies within the inner
  planes, no triangle comes within 1.2 mm of a frame item (rails, rods, stack reserve, harness, standoffs, brackets
  that stay) or of the bounding box of a part it does not hold (exact separating-axis test of each triangle against
  the boxes grown by 1.2 mm; the part's box re-derived from its STL file), none of those boxes lies inside the solid
  (winding number), and the solid touches every bolted plane it states under that part's footprint. Its distance to
  the parts it holds is **numeric** (FCL), listed, not proven.
* **Build volume:** checked against the 250 × 250 × 325 mm (ASSUMED) envelope in any axis-aligned orientation.

## Measured on L-2002

L-2002 is a 3U layout at 54 % part size, search budget 100, from the scratch project `v2verify` (designed v2.5
layout; read from its archive and design snapshot, nothing in `v2verify` modified). GEVS, AlSi10Mg.

**Machine load.** All M4 runs below were made on a shared, loaded PC: the user's V2 app was running on the same
machine (port 8766, project `default`, 6 search islands + 2 validators searching continuously since about 13:00 on
2026-10-24). My runs used at most 4 threads. Run times are therefore loaded-machine times, not benchmarks. Free memory
was the limit: 2.4–3.0 GB of the 16 GB were available during the runs (the coordinator measured about 0.6 GB at
13:10). Peak memory below is the resident memory of my process tree (the user's app's own use is not included).

  once). The other four metal-AM brackets (p08, p14 on −x; p16, p19 on −y) have no partner with clear space between.
  All four designed v2.5 layouts of `v2verify` (L-1988, L-1992, L-2002, L-2007) have such a five-part +y group.
* **Domain at 1.0 mm:** grid 76 × 13 × 89, 40 052 region voxels, 24 419 free, 10 086 design, 5 243 bolted; 151

  first rung), but its FE model has 411 537 dofs: NGSolve would need about 2.3 GB (measured coefficient below) with
  2.7 GB available and 2 GB to keep free, so the check was not run ("not solved"). At 1.25 mm: 233 769 dofs, about

### One piece on L-2002

* **Design space:** grid 36 × 36 × 85 voxels of 2.667 × 2.667 × 3.959 mm on the inner planes; 77 741 free voxels;
  17 parts held, none on a corner rail. Building it took 46.6 s (peak 228 MB). The optimiser's fine level would have
  268 206 dofs, the coarse level 39 828 (measured: 10 610 elements, 0.29 s per iteration, 434 MB).
* **Estimate shown before the run** (ASSUMED cost model): 216 s for one material-use step (optimiser 71 s, FEA
  78 s), about 1.5 GB, 96 k FE dofs.
* **Run 1** (328 s, peak 481 MB): every rung refused — the marching-cubes surface touched p14 (a cut voxel corner).
* **Run 2** (416 s, peak 526 MB): the fine optimiser level was skipped by the memory check (predicted 2.4 GB at
  9 MB per 1000 dofs, ASSUMED, with 2.6 GB available); the coarse level converged in 101 iterations. The solid was
  one piece without any join, 85.3 × 96.0 × 118.8 mm (fits the build volume), 0.335 mm from the parts it holds at
  the pads. Its mass, 351 g + 59 fasteners, is **not a result**: a fallback bug gave the coarse level's passive
  blocks' density to the fine design voxels in them (fixed; a test covers it). Every rung then failed meshing:
  subtracting p14 had left slivers (worst tetrahedron quality 6e-7 with every gmsh algorithm).
* **Run 3** with the slivers simplified away (manifold3d `simplify`, 0.01 mm): the process died after 179 s with a
  native heap corruption (0xC0000374), 4 s into the check; peak 515 MB. Offline on run 2's saved solid, `simplify`
  itself did not crash, but it left needle triangles and gmsh's tetrahedra got worse (quality 4e-10 to 1e-7). So
  `simplify` was removed and the surface is now pulled back from the parts instead of cut (above); the crash's
  exact site was not reproduced.
* **Run 4** (step by step) was stopped by the memory guard during the optimiser: available memory fell to 2182 MB
  (the guard's limit was 2200 MB; this process 472 MB).
* **Not yet run:** the one piece with the fixes (pull-back, the fallback fix). It needs at least 4 GB free to start
  (the coordinator's rule for large runs); the machine had 2.4–2.7 GB free from 14:50 on.

## Memory: how the runs protect the machine

* Every FE solve and the optimiser's fine level are checked before they start (`gd.layouts.mem_guard`): if the
  predicted need would leave less than 2 GB available (`T2S_MEM_RESERVE_MB`), the solve is not started and the
  design says "not solved: …" (a combined bracket is then tried on coarser voxels).
* The predicted need per 1000 dofs, measured on the p17 + p10 combined bracket (201 069 dofs, 4 threads):

  | Solver | Measured | Used |
  |---|---|---|
  | NGSolve sparse Cholesky, 18 cases (the whole job process) | 1099 MB (5.5 MB per 1000 dofs) | 5.5 |
  | CalculiX SPOOLES, 3 cases (ccx.exe) | 742 MB, 18.2 s (3.7) | 4.5 (fill-in grows faster than the dofs) |
  | CalculiX iterative Cholesky, 3 cases (process tree) | 441 MB, 25.6 s (1.2 above the 201 MB before) | 2.0 |
  | optimiser fine level (gd.simp) | 5.1 MB per 1000 dofs at 39 828 dofs | 9.0 (ASSUMED at ~270 k dofs) |

* When SPOOLES would not fit, CalculiX uses its iterative Cholesky solver. Its accuracy depends on the conditioning:
  within 9e-6 of NGSolve's displacements on that compact bracket, but 3.8e-3 of max |u| from SPOOLES on the slender
  test cantilever (200 × 10 × 6 mm). The agreement with NGSolve is recorded whenever it is used, and the static
  stress is the larger of the two solvers'.
* Other NGSolve solvers do not save memory here (measured on the same mesh): CG with BDDC peaked at 1189 MB (for
  quadratic tetrahedra every dof is a vertex or edge dof, so the coarse problem is the whole problem); `pardiso` and
  `umfpack` were stopped by the memory guard at 1901 MB and 1264 MB (still growing).

## Assumptions (ASSUMED)

* L-PBF build volume 250 × 250 × 325 mm (a typical metal L-PBF machine).
* Bridges: at least 3 mm shared width, gaps of at most 25 mm.
* The combined bracket's quasi-static level is the launch preset's level for its lightest held part, applied to all
  its parts at once (the largest load factor: conservative).
* The one piece's estimate of time and memory (cost model), and the optimiser's fine-level memory, 9 MB per 1000 dofs.
* Fastener mass per M3 screw with washer (`mfg.rules.FASTENER_MASS_KG`), as for the per-part brackets.

## Limitations (known, not hidden)

* **Not measured on L-2002 (memory):** the whole five-part combined bracket and the one piece with the fixes. The
  comparison above therefore has no mass, margin or frequency for those two columns; the two-part combined brackets
  are the measured evidence that the combined check works on this layout.
* **The one piece is exploratory:** coarse voxels (2.7–4 mm), a coarse-level design when the fine level does not fit,
  its distance to the parts it holds is numeric (FCL), not proven, and it was not FE-checked on a real layout.
* **Masses depend on the voxel size** (the bolted plate and contact skin are one voxel thick), so per-part and
  combined masses are only comparable at the same voxel size.
* A combined bracket's members keep their own bolt patterns; no bolts are added in the bridges.
* Only metal 3D-printed brackets are combined; brackets on a corner rail stay per part in the one piece.
